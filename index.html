Question 1: Biological and Technical Replicates 5 marks

A researcher investigates the nitrogen content of tomato leaves. Leaves are collected from three independent tomato plants receiving the same fertilizer treatment. Each leaf sample is analysed three times using the same laboratory instrument.

Explore the sampling structure

Questions

aIdentify the number of biological replicates. (1 mark)
3 — the three independent tomato plants.
bIdentify the number of technical measurements per biological replicate. (1 mark)
3 — each leaf sample is analysed three times.
cExplain why three measurements from the same leaf sample cannot be considered three biological replicates. (2 marks)
They come from the same plant / same sample, so they are not independent. They only reflect measurement (instrument) precision, not the natural variation between plants. Treating them as biological replicates is pseudoreplication and would underestimate experimental error.
dWhich type of replication is essential for making valid statistical inferences about treatment effects? (1 mark)
Biological replication (independent experimental units).

(1) Water temperature and (2) UV radiation.
bState the levels of each factor. (2 marks)
Temperature: 25°C, 30°C (2 levels). UV radiation: Low, High (2 levels).

Question 3: Identifying Experimental Components 8 marks

A researcher investigates the effects of three nitrogen-fixing bacterial species on the nitrogen content of tomato plants. Nine pots are used, with each bacterial species assigned to three pots. Pots are kept under similar environmental conditions.

The nine pots

Species 1Species 2Species 3

Click any pot: one pot = one tomato plant = one inoculation = one experimental unit.

3 treatments × 3 replicates = 9 experimental units.

Questions

aIdentify the independent variable. (1)
Bacterial species (type of nitrogen-fixing bacteria).
bIdentify the dependent variable. (1)
Nitrogen content of the tomato plants.
cIdentify the experimental unit. (2)
The pot (containing a tomato plant) — the unit to which a bacterial species is applied.
dHow many treatments are included? (1)
3 (one per bacterial species).
eHow many replications per treatment? (1)
3 pots per species.
fExplain why pots are considered experimental units. (2)
Each pot independently receives one treatment (one bacterial species), and pots can be assigned to treatments independently of one another. Any two pots could receive different species, so the pot is the smallest unit to which a treatment is applied.

Question 4: Experimental Unit vs Observational Unit 6 marks

Effect of different feeding regimes on fish growth.
  • Twelve fish tanks are used.
  • Each tank receives one of three feeding regimes.
  • Ten fish are maintained in each tank.
  • Individual fish body weights are measured after 30 days.

What are we counting?

Regime 1Regime 2Regime 3
12
tanks (experimental units)
4

Questions

aIdentify the experimental unit. (2)
The fish tank — the feeding regime is applied to the whole tank.
bIdentify the observational unit. (2)
The individual fish — body weight is measured on each fish.
cIdentify the response variable. (1)
Fish body weight (growth) after 30 days.
dExplain why the number of fish measured does not necessarily represent the number of independent experimental replicates. (1)
Fish in the same tank share the same treatment application and tank environment, so they are not independent. Replication is at tank level: 12 tanks ÷ 3 regimes = 4 replicates, not 120 fish (treating fish as replicates = pseudoreplication).

Question 5: Identifying Variables and Confounding Factors 6 marks

Effect of fertilizer on chilli plant growth. The researcher applies Fertilizer A to plants near a window, Fertilizer B to plants far from the window, and Fertilizer C to plants in the middle of the greenhouse. After four weeks, Fertilizer A produces the highest growth.

Greenhouse simulator

Press “Grow for 4 weeks” to see the results.

Questions

aIdentify the independent variable. (1)
Type of fertilizer (A, B, C).
bIdentify the dependent variable. (1)
Growth of chilli plants (e.g. plant height or biomass).
cIdentify a possible confounding variable. (1)
Light intensity / sunlight exposure (position relative to the window); temperature differences across the greenhouse are also acceptable.
dExplain why the results may be biased. (2)
Each fertilizer is tied to one location, so fertilizer effect is confounded with light. Plants near the window get more light and may grow better regardless of fertilizer — we cannot separate the effect of Fertilizer A from the effect of extra light.
eSuggest ONE way to improve the arrangement. (1)
Randomize fertilizer allocation across positions, or use blocking by distance from the window (each block — near, middle, far — contains all three fertilizers).
